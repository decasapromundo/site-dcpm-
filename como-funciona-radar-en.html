You don't have to work three jobs and still feel underpaid.

Two, three jobs, and by the end of the month the money barely covers the bills. What you actually want is to travel the world for work, and have your family see you as someone who made it.

Maybe you've said one of these things.

"I've wanted to get into the cruise world for so long. I should have done this ages ago."

"For almost two years I was chasing the dream of working on ships, but I was going about it the wrong way."

These are words from my students and from people who applied before you. None of them talk about lacking skill. They all talk about doing it alone, with no real plan: fifteen videos that contradict each other, a template resume, and no answers.

My mission is simple: nobody should need three jobs to pay the bills.

You don't have to be overqualified or fluent in English to work on a cruise ship. That belief keeps a lot of good people stuck in the same place, working twice as hard and earning half of what they're worth.

I know because that was me. Before boarding my first ship, I was working three jobs: waiter at two restaurants during the same week and a DJ at events on weekends. Adding it all up, it came to around R$1,800 to R$2,000 a month.

In 2016, I boarded a Costa ship in the lowest position in the restaurant, earning US$600 a month. I cried the entire first week and thought about giving up. I stayed for ten years, moved on to Celebrity, and worked my way up to Front Desk Supervisor at Disney Cruise Line.

In the beginning, it wasn't ambition I lacked. It was a method. I made dumb mistakes that nobody warned me about, and that path, already cleared of those mistakes, is exactly what I give you.

The RADAR path, phase by phase.

This isn't an English class and it's not another course to watch. It's a method with a beginning, a middle, and an end, and in each phase you walk away with something concrete in hand.

  1. 1

    Route

    We define your starting point, the entry-level position that makes sense for you, and which companies to apply to, in what order.

    You leave with: your target position, the list of companies, and the application order, in writing.

  2. 2

    Resume and Application

    The experience you already have, positioned the way a recruiter actually reads it. Without making anything up.

    You leave with: your ready-to-send resume in English and your first applications submitted.

  3. 3

    Interview

    This is the Professional Universe method: you organize your work stories and practice with real questions, in English. Interviews aren't memorized.

    You leave with: your story script and a completed mock interview, with corrections applied.

  4. 4

    Boarding

    English test, documents, and the company's next steps. You know what's coming before you get there.

    You leave with: your documents in order and every company step completed, up to the day you board.
